## 2. Thai desirability map

**How to read this table.** The **↑** column lists wordings that Thai respondents are likely to see as "what a good person does" (คนดี). The **↓** column lists wordings that read as a moral fault. The rewrite column gives each function's side a respectable reason and a visible cost of similar size.

**Rule of thumb from the evidence:**
- Thais become more direct with close people (B1).
- They criticise in private, not in public (A2).
- They describe themselves modestly (A4).
- Items on critiquing, questioning and abstraction are unreliable in Thai (A1).
- Discipline, learning, diligence and public-mindedness are the official virtues (A10).
- Anything self-centred or showy loads on an "Egotism" factor (D1).

Avoid abstract-vs-concrete wording, because those facets fail in Thai (A1: Concrete–Abstract .31, Experiential–Theoretical .26). Use concrete scenes. ✅ "เวลาคุยเรื่องแผนเที่ยวกับเพื่อน ฉันมักจะ… (ก) โยนไอเดียไปเรื่อย ๆ ว่ามีแบบไหนได้บ้าง แม้จะยังไม่ได้ข้อสรุป (ข) ช่วยเลือกจากตัวเลือกที่มีแล้วให้จบ แม้จะไม่ได้คิดทางเลือกใหม่ ๆ". |
| **Ni** (foresight, single insight) | "มองการณ์ไกล", "คิดถึงผลระยะยาว". These are ↑ and overlap with มีสติ and พอเพียง. | "เชื่อลางสังหรณ์", "ทำตามความรู้สึกลึก ๆ โดยอธิบายไม่ได้", "รู้ก่อนคนอื่น". These can read as งมงาย or as boastful (อวดรู้), which breaks the modesty norm (A4). | Avoid ability claims ("ฉันมักคาดถูก"), because modesty pushes them down (A4). Describe the *process* instead. ✅ "เวลามีเรื่องให้ตัดสินใจ ฉันมักจะ… (ก) นึกภาพไกล ๆ ว่าอีกหลายปีมันจะไปจบที่ไหน แม้จะอธิบายเหตุผลทีละข้อไม่ค่อยได้ (ข) ดูจากข้อมูลที่เห็นตรงหน้าตอนนี้ แม้จะไม่ได้คิดไปไกลมาก". |

**Cross-cutting rules (hypotheses drawn from A1–D2):**
1. **Do not set an item in a hierarchy.** Leave out parents, teachers, bosses, monks and elders. Kreng jai, katanyu and respect for elders (A2, A6, A10) decide the answer, not the function. Use peers, close friends, strangers, or being alone.
2. **Private beats public.** Any confrontation should happen in private or with a close friend (A2: 4% vs 68%; B1).
3. **Describe behaviour, not ability.** Claims like "I'm good at…" or "I usually get it right" are pushed down by modesty (A4).
4. **Attach a cost to each side** with a clause such as "แม้ว่า…". This makes neither side free.
5. **Do not translate Feeling as อารมณ์.** Use "ความรู้สึกของคนที่เกี่ยวข้อง" or "สิ่งที่สำคัญกับใจ". อารมณ์ carries the ↓ of ใช้อารมณ์ / ใจร้อน, which works against jai yen.
6. **Avoid negations and abstract-vs-concrete wordings** (D1, A1).
7. **Two-option forced choice with no midpoint is sensible.** It sidesteps the reported neutral and acquiescence tendencies (C2, C3), but desirability then moves into which option a person picks. Pilot each pair and drop any where one option gets more than about 75% (a suggested threshold, not drawn from the literature).

## 4. Honest gaps: what is evidence and what is hypothesis

**Evidence (data on Thai respondents)**
- Thai MBTI facet unreliability for Critical–Accepting, Questioning–Accommodating and the S–N facets, and the S/T/J skew. **Caveats:** a convenience sample of N = 240, 71% female, and an inconsistent N in the brief (A1).
- Private criticism is preferred over public criticism (A2, 1997).
- Indirect refusal is the norm, with more directness toward close people (B1, B2).
- Modest self-rating (A4, qualitative interviews with managers, not survey data).
- Mini-IPIP structure differs in a Southern Thai sample, with "partying/many people" carrying a negative connotation (D1, N = 212).
- E and A merge in IPIP-Thai (D2).
- Younger cohorts value Face and Conformity more (A9, abstract only).

**Hypotheses (my inference, not tested)**
- That Thai T and S/J skews are partly *desirability-driven*: "มีเหตุผล" and "มีวินัย/รับผิดชอบ" read as virtues. The brief itself offers culture, translation or carelessness as possible causes, and does not pick one.
- That every ↑/↓ wording in Section 2 behaves as described. No Thai study has rated the desirability of MBTI-type items. **A pilot is needed:** ask about 30 Thai raters "ข้อไหนคนส่วนใหญ่จะมองว่าดีกว่า" for each pair and keep pairs near 50/50.
- That avoiding hierarchy contexts removes most of the kreng jai bias.

**Not found or not verified**
- Komin's primary text (ranking known only from secondary summaries; [SEC]).
- **GLOBE country scores for Thailand.** No source opened in this session showed them, and the MIT Sloan page had no Thai numbers. Do not cite specific GLOBE values without checking House et al. (2004).
- Any Thai Marlowe-Crowne adaptation.
- Thai HEXACO validation (only Fischer's 4 added H items).
- Waiyavutti's 2019 results.
- Any peer-reviewed Thai study of midpoint or acquiescence specifically.
- The item text of Jantawarn's SDR scale.
- The full list of the 12 core values (from memory).

**Age of the evidence:** Komin (1990/91) and Verluyten (1997) are old. Newer sources (A9 2026, B1 2022, B2 2024, D1 2021) agree in direction on face, indirectness and conformity, but none measures the items directly.
