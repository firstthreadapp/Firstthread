# Desirability-balanced forced-choice items: evidence and working rules (r11)

Compiled 2026-10-16. Scope: a low-stakes Thai self-reflection quiz built entirely from two-option items (ก. vs ข.) on a 5-point preference scale. Items are cross-function pairs matched by the engine, hand-written bipolar items (Ni–Se, Ne–Si, Ti–Fe, Te–Fi), and E/I items.

## 1. Findings

### A. Evaluative neutralization (rewriting the item, not just matching it)

1. **Bäckström & Björklund (2013).** *Social desirability in personality inventories: Symptoms, diagnosis and prescribed cure.* Scand. J. Psychol. 54(2), 152–159. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3618383 **[FULL]**
   - **Diagnosis.** Socially desirable responding comes from the item's evaluative content interacting with the rater's motive to look good.
   - **Popularity as a warning sign.** An item's mean rating ("popularity") is a cheap flag. The correlation between item mean and the item's loading on social desirability was r ≈ .31–.62, and up to .78 after partialling out scale.
   - **Rewriting rules given to naive students:**
     1. Write an item you would find less desirable yourself.
     2. Swap a positive adjective for a less evaluative one, or rephrase the item.
     3. Keep the item's keyed direction.
     4. Check whether the item is reversed.
   - **Example.** "Love to help others" became "Have a need to help others."
   - **Results:**
     - 93 of 99 rewritten items were rated less desirable than the originals.
     - Mean correlation with Self-Deception fell from .43 to .24, and with Impression Management from .28 to .18.
     - Mean inter-scale r fell from .27 to .09.
     - The first principal component dropped from 27.8% to 19.8% of variance.
   - **Cost.** Reliability was somewhat lower (α .52–.85 vs .70–.88). New and old scales correlated .60–.84.

2. **Bäckström & Björklund (2020).** *The properties and utility of less evaluative personality scales.* Frontiers in Psychology 11. https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.560271 **[FULL, first ~70% read]**
   - **Rating scale for desirability.** 9 points: 1 = undesirable, 5 = neutral, 9 = desirable. The target is item means near the midpoint.
   - **Effect size.** Neutralized items were rated M = 5.67 vs 6.55 for the originals (d = 1.02).
   - **Examples:**
     - "Like order" became "Am only pleased if things are put in systematic order."
     - "Avoid contact with others" became "Feel at ease even with being alone."
   - **Validity.** Convergent validity was kept and discriminant validity improved.
   - **Translation.** Neutrality does not survive literal translation. The authors recommend calibrating items in each language against local midpoint means. **Implication: Thai items need Thai desirability ratings.**

3. **Bäckström, Björklund & Lindén (2014).** *Criterion validity is maintained when items are evaluatively neutralized.* Eur. J. Personality 28(6), 620–633. https://www.lunduniversity.lu.se/lup/publication/9e09524f-6ca8-4eb9-990d-e5d48cddd590 **[ABS]**
   - Criterion validity was equal for neutralized and original items.
   - Neutralized items were less tied to how desirable the criterion was.
   - The method is "a viable technique."

4. **Bäckström, Björklund & Larsson (2009).** J. Res. Personality 43(3), 335–344. doi:10.1016/j.jrp.2008.12.013 **[PAGE]**
   - This is the original demonstration: a general factor driven by social desirability shrinks when items are framed neutrally.

5. **Bäckström & Björklund (2023/2024).** *Why forced-choice and Likert items provide the same information on personality, including social desirability.* Educ. Psychol. Meas. 84(3), 549–576. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11095325/ **[FULL]**
   - **Key point for us:** the forced-choice format does not by itself remove social desirability when the statements are evaluative. Desirability carries into the choices. The cure is neutralized statements, which work in any format.
   - **Design advice:**
     - Never pair a highly desirable statement with a clearly undesirable one, because the undesirable one is almost never chosen.
     - Mix keying.
     - Use many blocks and rotate scale positions.
     - Simple summed scoring performed as well as SEM in their simulations.

### B. Desirability matching of forced-choice blocks: does it work, and how tightly?

6. **Frick (2022).** *Modeling faking in the MFC format: the Faking Mixture model.* Psychometrika 87(2), 773–794. doi:10.1007/s11336-021-09818-6. https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/A043532601D8D68104213D9706FC1E7F/S0033312300007948a.pdf/modeling-faking-in-the-multidimensional-forced-choice-format-the-faking-mixture-model.pdf **[FULL]**
   - **How desirability was rated.** Statements were rated by 33 students on a 5-point scale, using a short definition ("fulfilling societal norms and expectations") plus examples. Blocks were matched on these ratings.
   - **Result:** matching was "necessary but not sufficient." Some blocks that were matched on single-item ratings were still fakable. This is a context effect: two statements read side by side are judged differently than when each is read alone.
   - **Practical screen.** Tabulate how often each option or rank order is chosen under honest instructions vs. fake-good instructions.
     - For a well-matched block, fakers cannot agree on the "good" answer, so their choices spread out roughly uniformly.
     - For a poorly matched block, fakers converge on one option and the distribution is skewed.
     - Keep low-fakability blocks; replace the statement that stands out.
   - **Ways to estimate desirability (review section):**
     - Use item means from a previous administration.
     - Use the difference in item intercepts between honest and fake-good conditions (Lee et al., 2018; Ng et al., 2020).
     - Have an external panel rate the items, in general or for a specific scenario.

7. **Wetzel, Frick & Brown (2021).** *Does multidimensional forced-choice prevent faking?* Psychological Assessment 33(2), 156–170. doi:10.1037/pas0000971 **[PAGE]** (design also described in Frick 2022 [FULL])
   - Design: N = 1,867, within-person, honest vs. fake-good instructions.
   - Forced-choice reduced faking substantially compared with rating scales, but did not prevent it.
   - The version with desirability-matched triplets was faked less than a version where seven items were swapped for desirable ones.

8. **Li, Zhang, Li, Sun & Brown (2024).** *Mixed-keying or desirability-matching in the construction of forced-choice measures?* Organizational Research Methods. doi:10.1177/10944281241229784. https://tianjunsun.owlstown.net/publications/34793-mixed-keying-or-desirability-matching-in-the-construction-of-forced-choice-measures-an-empirical-investigation-and-practical-recommendations **[ABS]**
   - Four forced-choice forms were built from one statement pool.
   - **Stricter desirability matching meant more faking resistance.** More mixed-keyed blocks gave better convergent validity.
   - Both goals can be balanced. The paper includes a 7-step guide and an autoFC tutorial.

9. **Schulte, Kaup, Bürkner & Holling (2024).** *The fakeability of personality measurement with graded paired comparisons.* J. Business & Psychology 39(5), 1067–1084. doi:10.1007/s10869-024-09931-0 **[PAGE]** (Springer redirect and FU-Berlin 429 blocked access; abstract seen via search)
   - This is the closest match to our format: graded paired comparisons, Thurstonian IRT, N = 573, honest then applicant condition.
   - Score inflation was smaller than for Likert items but still present.
   - **Faking effects correlated almost perfectly with the desirability differences between paired statements.** This is the strongest argument that the size of the gap, not just its presence, matters.
   - The same group's ORM (2024) simulation found that about 50% unequally keyed pairs, or optimized loadings, avoided ipsativity. [PAGE]

10. **Okada, Furukawa & Bunji (2026).** *Quantifying and mitigating socially desirable responding in LLMs: a desirability-matched graded forced-choice study.* ACL 2026 / arXiv:2602.17262. https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.17262 (HTML read: https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17262) **[FULL, ~70% read]**
    - **Construction.** 30 cross-domain pairs on a 7-point bipolar scale (1 = left much more accurate, 7 = right much more accurate).
    - **Desirability ratings.** 9-point ratings on the normative "how desirable for an adult" framing.
    - **Matching.** A two-stage mixed-integer optimization:
      1. Minimize the largest within-pair gap (minimax).
      2. Then balance domain pairs and keying.
    - **Achieved gap:** max |Δ| = 0.18 and mean |Δ| = 0.03 on the 1–9 scale.
    - **Result.** Under fake-good instructions, 45 of 45 Likert cells shifted significantly; with graded forced-choice only 4 of 45 did. Profile recovery mostly stayed at r ≥ .50.
    - **Caveats.** The respondents were LLMs and the desirability raters were LLMs. The pairing method transfers to our engine; the effect sizes do not.

11. **Pavlov, Shi, Maydeu-Olivares & Fairchild (2021).** *Item desirability matching in forced-choice test construction.* Personality and Individual Differences 183, 111114. doi:10.1016/j.paid.2021.111114 **[PAGE]** (ScienceDirect 403)
    - The point, as cited by the autoFC manual: matching on panel means is not enough. Raters should also agree person by person that the two statements are equally desirable ("inter-item agreement", IIA). autoFC computes IIA with Gwet's AC and Brennan–Prediger indices.

12. **autoFC R package manual** (Li et al.). https://tspsyched.r-universe.dev/autoFC/doc/manual.html **[FULL]**
    - **How it matches.** A blueprint gives a per-block cutoff on the desirability range (max − min). If no combination fits, the cutoff is relaxed by ×1.25.
    - **No default threshold.** The manual's example uses a cutoff of 0.5 on a 1–5-type scale for illustration only.

13. **Cao & Drasgow (2019).** *Does forcing reduce faking? A meta-analysis.* J. Applied Psychology 104(11), 1347–1368. https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/does-forcing-reduce-faking-a-meta-analytic-review-of-forced-choic/ **[ABS]**
    - Overall score inflation for forced-choice was d ≈ 0.06.
    - **Moderator:** blocks balanced on desirability were more faking-resistant.
    - Job-relevant facets inflated more. The analogue for us: facets that feel "relevant to being a good person or thinker" will inflate more.
    - Speer et al. (2023, JAP 108, 1812–1833) is a newer meta-analysis on the same question. **[PAGE]**

14. **Chawota, Speer, Tenbrink, Wegmeyer & Delacruz (2026).** *It's all relative: ipsativity on forced-choice tests* (meta-analysis, 49 samples). IJSA. doi:10.1111/ijsa.70053. https://bearworks.missouristate.edu/articles00/36 **[ABS]**
    - Equal-desirability blocks produce more ipsativity: scores are relative within the person. This goes with lower construct validity but more resistance to faking.
    - **For us:** an 8-function profile is meant to be relative, so this trade-off is acceptable. Do not present results as normative ("you are more Ni than other people").

15. **Kleinbub & Seitz (2026).** *When perceptions of social desirability differ.* Applied Psychological Measurement. doi:10.1177/01466216261460893. https://madoc.bib.uni-mannheim.de/72569/1/kleinbub-seitz-2026-when-perceptions-of-social-desirability-differ-implications-for-the-multidimensional-nominal.pdf **[FULL, intro + refs]**
    - People differ in which statements they see as desirable ("idiographic desirability"; Ludeke et al., 2013).
    - Desirability is often not monotone across response categories. The most desirable answer can be a moderate one or even the midpoint (Kuncel & Tellegen, 2009, Personnel Psychology 62, 201–228 **[PAGE]**). Under "look good" instructions, people often pick "agree" rather than "strongly agree."

### C. Item transparency and wording

16. **Vidotto, Anselmi, Filipponi, Tommasi & Saggino (2018).** *Using overt and covert items in self-report personality tests.* Frontiers in Psychology 9, 1100. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6037895 **[FULL]**
    - Both kinds of item could be faked, but overt items (construct obvious) were faked more.
    - Under faking, overt items measured a different construct: the item–measure correlation was r = .40 for overt items vs .92 for covert ones.
    - Misfit statistics flagged 35% of fakers on overt items vs 19% on covert items.
    - **Lesson:** do not name the construct, or its flattering label, inside the item.

17. **Liu & Zhang (2020).** *Item-level analysis for detecting faking: ideal point IRT.* Frontiers in Psychology 10, 3090. https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03090/full **[FULL summary]**
    - Under fake-good instructions, 18.8% of conscientiousness items and 50% of neuroticism items shifted location.
    - Conscientiousness items shifted uniformly upward. Neuroticism items shifted in both directions because their desirability is ambiguous.
    - **Lesson:** fake-good item shifts are a usable empirical desirability index. Ambiguous-desirability content is closer to neutral.

### D. The midpoint ("equally" / พอๆ กัน)

18. **Kankaraš & Capecchi (2025).** *Neither agree nor disagree: use and misuse of the neutral response category in Likert-type scales.* METRON 83(1), 111–140. https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/metron/v83y2025i1d10.1007_s40300-024-00276-5.html **[ABS]**
    - Twelve personality scales were tested with and without a midpoint. Scales with a midpoint were somewhat more reliable and had a stronger first factor.
    - Most respondents use the midpoint validly. A minority use it as an escape, especially on socially sensitive items.

19. **Wetzelhütter (2020).** *Scale-sensitive response behavior!? Offering vs omitting a "don't know" option and/or a middle category.* Survey Practice 13(1). https://www.surveypractice.org/article/14484.pdf **[FULL, partial]**
    - Respondents who choose the middle category are not necessarily undecided.
    - The literature is mixed on whether a midpoint helps or hurts data quality.
    - Sturgis, Roberts & Smith (2014) found many midpoint answers are "face-saving don't knows." **[PAGE]**

20. **Brown & Maydeu-Olivares (2018).** *Ordinal factor analysis of graded-preference questionnaire data.* Structural Equation Modeling. https://kar.kent.ac.uk/63990/ **[ABS]**
    - Graded paired comparisons can be modeled as ordinal CFA / Thurstonian IRT.
    - Graded blocks matched Likert when items were well differentiated, and may be superior when acquiescence, halo or desirability blur the differences.
    - The page does not say whether a middle category was used.

21. **Abad, Schames Kreitchmann, Graña, Nájera & Sorrel (2025).** *Pruebas de elección forzosa: visión actual y recomendaciones.* Acción Psicológica 22(1), 57–72. https://revistas.uned.es/index.php/accionpsicologica/article/view/43413 **[ABS]**
    - A step-by-step construction guide, with TIRT/MUPP scoring, item polarity and R code.
    - Lee, Son, Zhou, Joo, Jia & Cheng (2025, ORM 28(4), 680–722) is a review of 80 years of forced-choice research. **[PAGE]**

**What the evidence agrees on:**
1. Forced-choice reduces but does not remove desirability bias, because desirability carries into the choice.
2. The size of the bias tracks the size of the within-pair desirability gap (Schulte 2024; Wetzel 2021; Li 2024; Cao & Drasgow 2019).
3. Single-item ratings are necessary but not sufficient, so you need an empirical check of the pair in context (Frick 2022).
4. The most robust fix is to neutralize the statements themselves, then match them (Bäckström & Björklund 2013/2020/2023).
5. Desirability norms are culture-specific, so calibrate in Thai.

## 2. ITEM CHECKLIST (testable rules)

Each rule is phrased so a reviewer, or a regex plus a human, can pass or fail it. ✗ = bad item, ✓ = fixed item.

**R1. No ability or competence words on either side.** Describe a preference or habit, never a skill level.
- ✗ ก. ฉันเก่งในการมองภาพรวมและคาดการณ์อนาคต / ข. ฉันสังเกตรายละเอียดรอบตัวได้ดี
- ✓ ก. เวลาเริ่มงานใหม่ ฉันมักนึกภาพปลายทางก่อน แล้วค่อยย้อนคิดว่าต้องทำอะไร / ข. เวลาเริ่มงานใหม่ ฉันมักลงมือจากสิ่งที่อยู่ตรงหน้าก่อน แล้วค่อยปรับตามที่เห็น

**R2. No virtue or moral words.** No ใส่ใจ, เสียสละ, ยุติธรรม, มีเหตุผล and similar. This is the main risk for Fe and Ti, because both labels are flattering in Thai.
- ✗ ก. ฉันใส่ใจความรู้สึกของคนรอบข้าง / ข. ฉันตัดสินใจอย่างมีเหตุผล
- ✓ ก. ถ้าบรรยากาศในวงคุยเริ่มตึง ฉันมักพาเปลี่ยนเรื่องให้ทุกคนสบายใจก่อน แม้ประเด็นจะยังไม่จบ / ข. ถ้าบรรยากาศในวงคุยเริ่มตึง ฉันมักถามต่อจนเหตุผลลงตัว แม้บางคนจะเริ่มอึดอัด

**R3. Costs are symmetric.** If one side carries a cost clause (แม้…, ถึงจะ…), the other side carries one of similar weight. Otherwise, neither does. A one-sided cost makes that side heroic or makes the other side look lazy.
- ✗ ก. ฉันช่วยงานเพื่อนก่อนแม้ตัวเองจะเหนื่อย / ข. ฉันทำงานของตัวเองให้เสร็จก่อน
- ✓ ก. ถ้าเพื่อนขอให้ช่วย ฉันมักช่วยทันที แล้วงานตัวเองก็เลื่อนออกไป / ข. ถ้าเพื่อนขอให้ช่วย ฉันมักขอปิดงานตัวเองก่อน แล้วเพื่อนก็ต้องรอ

**R4. Same frequency or hedge word on both sides, and no absolutes.** Ban เสมอ, ทุกครั้ง, ไม่เคย, ตลอด. Use มัก or ส่วนใหญ่ on both sides.
- ✗ ก. ฉันวางแผนล่วงหน้าเสมอ / ข. บางครั้งฉันก็ทำไปตามสถานการณ์
- ✓ ก. ก่อนเที่ยว ฉันมักจองที่พักและวางเส้นทางไว้ล่วงหน้า / ข. ก่อนเที่ยว ฉันมักกำหนดแค่ปลายทาง แล้วไปตัดสินใจที่หน้างาน

**R5. Use one shared situation stem with concrete behavior, not trait adjectives.** Both options answer the same situation.
- ✗ ก. ฉันเป็นคนมีจินตนาการ / ข. ฉันเป็นคนติดดิน
- ✓ (stem) เวลาอ่านนิยายหรือดูหนัง… ก. ฉันมักคิดต่อว่าเรื่องนี้สื่อถึงอะไรหรือจะจบอย่างไร / ข. ฉันมักจดจำฉาก บรรยากาศ และรายละเอียดที่เห็นได้ชัด

**R6. Do not name or label the construct, so the item stays low in transparency.** Ban สัญชาตญาณ, ตรรกะ, ความรู้สึก (as a label), วิสัยทัศน์, หลักการ, and words close to the function names.
- ✗ ก. ฉันตัดสินใจด้วยตรรกะ / ข. ฉันตัดสินใจด้วยความรู้สึก
- ✓ ก. ก่อนตัดสินใจเรื่องสำคัญ ฉันมักเขียนข้อดีข้อเสียออกมาเทียบกัน / ข. ก่อนตัดสินใจเรื่องสำคัญ ฉันมักถามตัวเองว่าทางไหนที่ทำแล้วรู้สึกว่า "ใช่"

**R7. No outcome or success words.** Describe the process, not the result. Ban สำเร็จ, ได้ผลดี, ถูกต้อง, มีประสิทธิภาพ, ได้เร็ว.
- ✗ ก. ฉันจัดระบบงานจนทีมทำงานได้มีประสิทธิภาพ / ข. ฉันทำงานตามคุณค่าที่ตัวเองเชื่อ
- ✓ ก. เมื่อทำงานกลุ่ม ฉันมักเป็นคนแบ่งงาน ตั้งเส้นตาย และตามความคืบหน้า / ข. เมื่อทำงานกลุ่ม ฉันมักเลือกรับส่วนที่ตรงกับสิ่งที่ตัวเองให้ความสำคัญ แม้จะไม่ใช่ส่วนที่เร่งที่สุด

**R8. No social-approval anchors.** No คนอื่นชมว่า…, เป็นที่รัก, ได้รับการยอมรับ, เป็นที่พึ่ง.
- ✗ ก. เพื่อนมักมาปรึกษาฉันเพราะฉันเข้าใจคนอื่น / ข. ฉันชอบใช้เวลากับความคิดของตัวเอง
- ✓ ก. เวลาว่าง ฉันมักทักหรือโทรหาเพื่อนเพื่อคุยเรื่องทั่วไป / ข. เวลาว่าง ฉันมักอยู่กับความคิดหรือกิจกรรมของตัวเองโดยไม่ทักใคร

**R9. No one-sided negative self-labels.** If one side contains ใจร้อน, ดื้อ, เย็นชา, เพ้อฝัน, ขี้ลืม and the like, rewrite it. Tagging only one side with a flaw guarantees that side loses.
- ✗ ก. ฉันมักเพ้อฝันถึงความเป็นไปได้ต่างๆ / ข. ฉันยึดกับสิ่งที่เคยทำมาแล้วได้ผล
- ✓ ก. เมื่อมีวิธีใหม่ให้ลอง ฉันมักอยากลองแม้จะยังไม่รู้ว่าได้ผลไหม / ข. เมื่อมีวิธีใหม่ให้ลอง ฉันมักใช้วิธีเดิมที่คุ้นเคยต่อไป แม้วิธีใหม่อาจดีกว่า

**R10. Same length, number of clauses and level of abstraction.** Character counts should be within ±20%. If one side is concrete and the other abstract, the vivid side wins.
- ✗ ก. ฉันชอบคิดถึงความหมายเบื้องหลังสิ่งต่างๆ / ข. ฉันชอบทำอาหาร จัดบ้าน และดูแลต้นไม้ในวันหยุด
- ✓ ก. วันหยุด ฉันมักใช้เวลาอ่านหรือคิดเรื่องที่ไม่มีคำตอบตายตัว / ข. วันหยุด ฉันมักใช้เวลาทำงานบ้านหรืองานมือที่เห็นผลทันที

**R11. Neutralize Thai cultural virtues.** เกรงใจ, กตัญญู, รู้กาลเทศะ, ถ่อมตัว, and keeping harmony are strongly desirable in Thai, so Fe and Si items using them will be over-chosen. Describe the behavior together with its trade-off. (This is an inference from Bäckström & Björklund 2020 on culture-specific desirability; I did not open a Thai study.)
- ✗ ก. ฉันเกรงใจผู้ใหญ่และไม่ค่อยเถียง / ข. ฉันพูดตรงๆ ถ้าคิดว่าเรื่องนั้นไม่ถูก
- ✓ ก. ถ้าไม่เห็นด้วยกับผู้ใหญ่ในวง ฉันมักเก็บไว้ก่อน แล้วอาจไม่ได้พูดเลย / ข. ถ้าไม่เห็นด้วยกับผู้ใหญ่ในวง ฉันมักพูดออกไปตรงนั้น แม้บรรยากาศจะกร่อย

**R12. No normative or ideal-self modals.** Ban ควร, อยากเป็นคนที่…, พยายาม… on one side. Use the same mood (habitual มัก…) on both sides, and optionally anchor to a recent time window.
- ✗ ก. ฉันพยายามมองโลกในแง่ดีและหาโอกาสใหม่ๆ / ข. ฉันทำตามขั้นตอนที่ควรทำ
- ✓ ก. ในช่วงหลายเดือนที่ผ่านมา ฉันมักเริ่มโปรเจกต์ใหม่หลายอย่างพร้อมกัน / ข. ในช่วงหลายเดือนที่ผ่านมา ฉันมักทำโปรเจกต์ที่มีอยู่ให้จบก่อนเริ่มอันใหม่

**R13. One idea per side (no double-barrel).** Each side expresses one behavior, so a person who does only half of it is not stuck.
- ✗ ก. ฉันชอบพบปะผู้คนและเป็นผู้นำในกลุ่ม / ข. ฉันชอบอยู่เงียบๆ
- ✓ ก. หลังสัปดาห์ที่วุ่นวาย ฉันมักฟื้นแรงด้วยการนัดเจอเพื่อน แม้จะไม่มีเวลาจัดการเรื่องตัวเอง / ข. หลังสัปดาห์ที่วุ่นวาย ฉันมักฟื้นแรงด้วยการอยู่คนเดียว แม้จะพลาดนัดสังสรรค์บางงาน

**R14. Each statement sits in the neutral band before pairing.**
- **Rule.** The panel mean on a 1–9 scale (1 = น่าตำหนิ, 5 = กลางๆ, 9 = น่าชื่นชม) must fall between 3.5 and 6.5. Statements outside the band are rewritten (Bäckström: target near the midpoint). Two statements that are both very desirable are "matched" but still invite fine-grained impression management (Frick 2022).
- ✗ ก. ฉันพร้อมรับฟังทุกความเห็นอย่างเปิดใจ (≈8) / ข. ฉันวิเคราะห์ปัญหาอย่างเป็นระบบ (≈8)
- ✓ ก. เวลาคนเสนอความเห็นต่าง ฉันมักถามต่อว่าเขาคิดแบบนั้นเพราะอะไร / ข. เวลาคนเสนอความเห็นต่าง ฉันมักเอาไปเทียบกับข้อมูลที่มีก่อนจะตอบ

**R15. The pair passes the empirical gap test.**
- **Rule.** The within-pair desirability gap is at most 0.5 on 1–9 (at most 0.25 on 1–5), and the pair passes the fake-good pilot in §4.
- ✗ "ฉันมักให้อภัยคนง่าย" (7.4) paired with "ฉันมักจำได้ว่าใครเคยทำอะไรกับฉัน" (3.6), Δ = 3.8
- ✓ "ฉันมักลืมเรื่องที่คนเคยทำให้ไม่พอใจไปเร็ว" (5.3) paired with "ฉันมักจำรายละเอียดของเรื่องที่เคยเกิดขึ้นได้นาน" (5.6), Δ = 0.3

**How to neutralize** (Bäckström-style):

- Turn an avoidance into an ease: "ไม่ชอบเข้าสังคม" becomes "รู้สึกสบายดีแม้อยู่คนเดียวทั้งวัน" (cf. "Feel at ease even with being alone").

## 4. Recommended matching tolerance and screening thresholds

These are design heuristics derived from the sources. No source prescribes a universal cutoff; autoFC explicitly leaves the cutoff to the user.

**Stage 0: rate each statement on its own (before pairing)**
- **Panel.** At least 30 Thai raters who resemble the target users. Frick used 33 raters.
- **Question.** "คนทั่วไปจะมองว่าการเป็นแบบนี้ น่าชื่นชมหรือน่าตำหนิแค่ไหน". 9 points, 1 = น่าตำหนิมาก, 5 = กลางๆ, 9 = น่าชื่นชมมาก, following Bäckström and Okada.
- **Neutral band.** Keep statements with a mean between 3.5 and 6.5. Rewrite anything above 7 or below 3.
- **Rater agreement (Pavlov).** Also compute each rater's own difference for every candidate pair (rating A − rating B). Require that at least 60% of raters put the two statements within ±1 point of each other.
  - **Why:** equal means can hide a split in which half the raters prefer A and half prefer B.

**Stage 1: engine pairing tolerance**
- **Gap thresholds (1–9 scale):**

  | |Δmean| | Action |
  |---|---|
  | ≤ 0.5 | Accept |
  | 0.5–1.0 | Pairable only if the pilot in Stage 2 passes |
  | > 1.0 | Never pair |

- **On a 1–5 scale:** ≤ 0.25 accept, ≤ 0.5 conditional.
- **Why 0.5:**
  - With n = 30 raters and a rating SD of about 1.5, the standard error of each mean is about 0.27. The standard error of a difference is about 0.39, so a gap below about 0.5 cannot be told apart from noise. A tighter cutoff needs a larger panel.
  - Schulte et al. (2024) found that faking effects track the gap almost linearly, so smaller is better.
  - Okada et al. (2026) show an optimizer can reach max |Δ| ≈ 0.2 when the pool is large.
- **Pair selection.** Use a minimax rule: minimize the worst gap first, then the mean gap (Okada).
- **Keying.** Mix keying so that about half the pairs are not "both positive" (Schulte ORM 2024; Li 2024). Rotate which function appears as ก. vs ข.

**Stage 2: fake-good pilot** (the empirical check of each pair in context; Frick 2022, Wetzel 2021)
- **Setup.** N ≥ 100, within-person: honest instruction, then "ตอบให้ตัวเองดูดีที่สุดในสายตาคนอื่น". Code the 5-point scale as −2..+2.
- **Fail** a pair if either of these holds:
  1. The mean shift from honest to fake-good is at least 0.5 scale points, or Cohen's d ≥ 0.3.
  2. Under fake-good, the share choosing one side (midpoint excluded) falls outside 35–65%.
     - **Why:** Frick's logic is that a well-matched pair leaves fakers with no consensus, so their choices approach 50/50.
- **Desirability index.** The fake-good mean is also a direct, in-context desirability index (the intercept-difference method of Lee et al. 2018 and Ng et al. 2020). Prefer it to panel ratings when the two disagree.

**Stage 3: post-launch monitoring** (real users, low stakes)
- **Choice share** (midpoint excluded):

  | Share choosing one side | Action |
  |---|---|
  | Outside 25–75% | Review the item |
  | Outside 20–80% | Rewrite the item |

  - Exception: E/I and some bipolar items can legitimately skew if the population really is skewed. Compare against the pilot honest-condition share before rewriting.
- **Thurstonian IRT intercept.** If the quiz is scored with Thurstonian IRT, an intercept whose implied choice probability lies outside about .20–.80 for a person with average trait levels is the same flag.
- **Impression management.** Add 4–6 short Thai impression-management items, optionally and not scored for the user. Flag any pair whose signed choice correlates |r| ≥ .20 with the impression-management score; rewrite at |r| ≥ .30.
  - Benchmark: neutralization brought mean correlations with impression management from .28 down to .18 (Bäckström 2013).
- **Midpoint use per item:**
  - Above 40%: the item is ambiguous, or the escape option is being used on a sensitive item (Kankaraš & Capecchi). Rewrite.
  - Below 5%: the pair is so polarized that the midpoint is never needed. Check for a desirability gap.
- **Midpoint use per person.** If a person chooses the midpoint on more than 50% of items, show a low-confidence note on their result.
- **Keep the item bank healthy.** After every 500 completions, re-run the screens and retire the worst 10% of pairs.

**Midpoint ("พอๆ กัน"): verdict for this quiz**
- **Keep it.** The quiz is low-stakes and reflective. A midpoint slightly improves reliability (Kankaraš & Capecchi) and avoids forcing truly indifferent people into a side.
- **Label it** "ทั้งสองแบบพอๆ กัน", not "ไม่แน่ใจ", so it means equality rather than "don't know".
- **Score it as 0.**
- **Costs to watch:**
  - It is an escape route on socially sensitive pairs.
  - It can hide non-opinions (Sturgis 2014).
  - Under a self-presentation motive, the "safe" answer is often a moderate one (Kuncel & Tellegen 2009).
  - The per-item and per-person midpoint flags above handle these.
- **Fallback.** If midpoint use averages above 35% across items, consider a 4-point scale (ก.มาก / ก.เล็กน้อย / ข.เล็กน้อย / ข.มาก). Pilot it first.
