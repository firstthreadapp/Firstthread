## The 8 functions (definitions to write against — follow Jung/Myers type-dynamics usage)
Perceiving (how information is taken in):
- **Se (extraverted sensing)** — attention on the immediate, concrete, sensory present; acts and adjusts in real time;
  notices what is physically there now; enjoys vivid experience; responds to what happens.
- **Si (introverted sensing)** — compares the present with stored personal experience; detailed recall of how things were
  and felt; trusts what has worked; routines and familiar references; awareness of inner bodily state.
- **Ne (extraverted intuition)** — sees many possibilities and connections in the outer world; brainstorming, "what if",
  branching ideas, novelty, jumping between linked ideas, potentials in people/things.
- **Ni (introverted intuition)** — converges on one underlying pattern/insight; a sense of where things are heading;
  symbolic/deeper meaning; long-range single vision; insight that "arrives" after incubation.
Judging (how decisions are made):
- **Te (extraverted thinking)** — organises the outer world logically: goals, plans, metrics, evidence, efficiency,
  decisive structuring of people/resources, cutting what doesn't work.
- **Ti (introverted thinking)** — internal logical consistency; precise definitions; building one's own framework;
  understanding *why* something works; spotting contradictions; independent analysis.
- **Fe (extraverted feeling)** — attunes to others' feelings and group harmony; shared values and social norms;
  accommodating, including, smoothing tension, expressing care in ways others appreciate.
- **Fi (introverted feeling)** — inner personal values and authenticity; "is this really me / right for me";
  private depth of feeling; holding a personal line even against the group.

## Writing rules (statements and sides)
1. Thai, first person "ฉัน", everyday spoken-polite register, ONE sentence, ideally 40–110 characters, max ~130.
2. Describe a habitual way I perceive / decide / act *inside this context*, not "I like topic X".
3. One function per statement. Don't smuggle a second function (e.g. an Ni item that is really "I plan schedules" = Te).
4. Social desirability must be close to neutral (target 3.0–3.75 on 1–5). Functions that sound virtuous (Fe, Fi, Te, Ni)
   often need a small, realistic cost ("แม้…", "จนบางที…"). Functions that sound weaker (Si, Se) must not sound lazy or shallow.
   Never use ability/performance words that make a side obviously better: เก่ง, ได้ดี, แม่น, ฉลาด, รู้ได้ทันที, เหนือกว่า.
5. Keep function and E/I separate: Fe ≠ being outgoing; Se ≠ partying; Ti/Ni ≠ being quiet. Avoid "คนเยอะ/อยู่คนเดียว" in function items.
6. No gender/age stereotypes, no mental-health wording, no money/class judgement, no religion judgement.
7. Thai spacing conventions as in the bank: "ใหม่ ๆ", quotes “…”.
8. Bipolar sides: same situation, same length (±20%), both start in parallel; a reader of either type should be able to
   pick their side without feeling it is the "worse person" answer.
9. Novel content: don't paraphrase an existing item of the same function/context.
