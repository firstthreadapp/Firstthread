# rev12: Which new contexts would improve measurement? (theory-based research)

Scope: a Thai forced-choice self-report questionnaire on Jungian cognitive functions (Ni Ne Si Se Ti Te Fi Fe, plus E/I). Each item pairs two statements from different functions in one everyday context. Respondents choose which contexts they answer.
Method: live web research done 2026-10-16. Every claim tagged [Rn] points to the REFS section at the end, which lists only URLs that were actually opened and read. A few sources could only be seen as search snippets (paywall, 403 or 429 errors). They are listed separately as "seen, not opened" and are not used as primary support.

---

## 0. Key takeaways from the theory (why contexts matter at all)

1. **Trait activation.** A trait shows up only when the situation offers cues relevant to it. Trait–behaviour correlations were highest when the scenario matched the trait (Tett & Guterman 2000). Cues come from task, social and organizational levels, and act as demands, distracters, constraints, releasers or facilitators (Tett & Burnett 2003) [R1].
   - A later review coded 262 moderator effects from 75 studies; about 60% were significant [R1 search abstract; see "seen, not opened"].
   - Implication: a context is useful only if it carries cues for both functions in a pair. A context with no people in it cannot activate Fe. A context with no system or task in it cannot activate Te.
2. **Relevance and strength are separate.** Tett & Burnett's radio analogy: trait relevance is the station, situational strength is the volume [R1].
   - A context can be relevant to a function and still be "loud": so strongly normed that everyone gives the same answer.
3. **Situational strength.** Strong situations restrict how individual differences are expressed, and so restrict their validity (Meyer, Dalal & Hermida 2010). The review gives four facets [R2][R3]:
   - **clarity:** expectations are obvious;
   - **consistency:** cues all point the same way;
   - **constraints:** choices are limited from outside;
   - **consequences:** the outcome matters to someone.
   - Judge & Zapata (2015) found that **all five** Big Five traits predicted performance better in weak work situations, meaning unstructured work with discretion. They also found trait-specific activation effects. For example, agreeableness was *less positively* related to performance in competitive contexts [R17].
   - For us, this means rating every candidate context on the four facets. Low on all four = good.
4. **Frame-of-reference (FOR).** Adding a context raises validity. Lievens et al. (2008) explain the mechanism: it mainly reduces within-person inconsistency, meaning each person stops imagining a different situation each time they answer. The frame must also be conceptually relevant [R23].
   - Contextualized measures out-predict generic ones (Shaffer & Postlethwaite 2012, as cited in [R15]).
   - Fully rewritten items beat tagged items, which beat instruction-only framing (Holtrop et al. 2014 [R14]; Holtrop & Oostrom 2025 review [R22]). Respondents also react best to fully contextualized items [R22].
   - Caveat: unintended context in an item can "spread" and distort a facet's meaning ("hidden framings", Schulze et al. 2021 [R11]).
   - Implication: each context should be distinct enough to give a clear, shared frame, and the frame should not drag in a confound (e.g., conscientiousness, agreeableness, expertise).
5. **Personality varies by domain within a person.** These sources [R6][R7][R10][R19][R21] show it:
   - Matz & Harari found that places predict momentary Big Five states, e.g., people felt more extraverted at cafés, bars and friends' houses than at home [R7].
   - Kuper et al. (2022) found that people differ reliably in how DIAMONDS situation characteristics relate to their states [R6].
   - Nasello et al. (2024) gave the BFI in five frames: work, family, friends, partner and leisure [R19].
     - Extraversion was lowest at work.
     - Conscientiousness was highest at work and in hobbies.
     - Agreeableness was lower with family and partner.
     - Openness was stable across all five frames.
   - Implication: contexts are not interchangeable. The context set changes the mean function profile. The set should therefore be **balanced**, so that no function is always measured in contexts that favour it.
6. **Situation taxonomies give a coverage checklist** [R5][R16]:
   - **DIAMONDS:** Duty, Intellect, Adversity, Mating, pOsitivity, Negativity, Deception, Sociality.
   - **CAPTION:** Complexity, Adversity, Positive valence, Typicality, Importance, humOr, Negative valence.
   - **Situational Six:** Negativity, Positivity, Familiarity, Demandingness, Oddness, Straightforwardness.
   - The only non-Western test (Korea, N = 363) found the dimensions collapse at the top level into **Tasks / Positivity / Negativity** [R5].
7. **MBTI axes overlap with the Big Five.** McCrae & Costa (1989) found that the four MBTI indices measure four of the Big Five [R8]. Furnham et al. (2003) replicated this [R9]:
   - E–I with Extraversion;
   - S–N with Openness;
   - T–F with Agreeableness;
   - J–P with Conscientiousness.
   - This is the main source of desirability risk. A context where agreeableness is the norm (Thai harmony, kreng jai) pulls answers toward F/Fe. A context where conscientiousness is the norm (work, health, money) pulls answers toward J/Te/Si.
8. **Thai cultural loading.** Komin's value clusters as summarized in [R18b]:
   - ego/face-saving first;
   - grateful-relationship (reciprocity, gratitude toward parents and benefactors) second;
   - smooth interpersonal relationships ("no-conflict") third;
   - Religio-psychical orientation present;
   - achievement-task orientation ranked lowest.
   - Kreng jai = consideration, deference and reluctance to impose. People may decline what they want, or keep smiling when something is unfair, to keep the peace [R18].
   - Implication: contexts centred on **obligation toward family/elders, religion, or interpersonal friction** are strong situations for Thai respondents, with Fe as the "good person" answer. This is the same reason the owner's "refusing" context was rejected.

## 1. Principles for choosing a context (operational definitions)

Score each principle 1–5 (5 = best).

| # | Principle | Operational test | Theory |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | **Weak situation** | Low on all four Meyer facets: (a) no obvious "right" behaviour (clarity); (b) people around you don't send one consistent expectation (consistency); (c) you choose freely (constraints); (d) a "wrong" choice costs little (consequences). Quick check: would 20 people behave visibly differently here? | [R2][R3][R17] |
| P2 | **Activates both perceiving and judging (all 8 writable)** | You can write a natural, non-strained statement for each of Se/Si/Ne/Ni *and* Te/Ti/Fe/Fi. Minimum: the context has (i) information to take in (sensory or abstract), (ii) a choice or evaluation to make, (iii) a people element (for Fe), and (iv) a thing or system element (for Te/Ti). Bonus if it has both a social and a solitary mode (for E/I). | [R1] trait relevance |
| P3 | **Low social-desirability pressure** | Neither pole of a likely pairing is the "good person" or "competent adult" answer in Thai culture. Red flags: duty to elders or family, religion and merit, open conflict, health virtue, money prudence, punctuality. The context should keep both Big Five correlates (A for T/F, C for J/P) neutral. | [R8][R9][R18][R18b] |
| P4 | **Common across ages and incomes** | Most Thai adults (roughly 15–70), in both cities and provinces and at both low and high income, have direct, recent experience of it. Items can be written without assuming money, a car, a partner, an office job or schooling. | [R13][R20] |
| P5 | **Not redundant** | The frame is distinct from existing contexts, so a respondent would not picture the same scene. It also adds a missing taxonomy cell, e.g., Typicality/Familiarity, Humor or mild Adversity. | [R5][R16][R23] |

Design rules that follow:
- **R-a. Write items as full context, not tags.** For example, "When I rearrange my room I…", not "(Home) I…" [R14][R22].
- **R-b. Balance contexts, not only items.** Each function should appear in contexts that favour it *and* in contexts that don't. Otherwise function scores depend on which contexts the respondent picked (personality varies by domain [R19][R7]).
- **R-c. Put adversity inside domains rather than as a domain.** The taxonomies say Negativity/Adversity is a basic dimension [R5][R16]. The rev11 list is almost entirely Positivity/Duty/Intellect/Sociality. But "arguing" as a whole context is a strong, A-loaded situation. The better route is to give *every* domain one or two "small mishap" items (a plan falls through, a thing breaks, a show you chose is bad). These must be non-interpersonal mishaps, so the T/F pole doesn't become an agreeableness test.
- **R-d. Respondent choice of context is good for trait activation** (people choose domains where they get relevant cues). It is bad for comparability: different people answer different item sets. Item parameters need to be calibrated so that the context does not shift the function scale (test for differential item functioning (DIF) by context).
- **R-e. Desirability-match the two statements within each pair, per context.** Matching is context-dependent. A pair that is matched in "music" may not be matched in "money". In the forced-choice literature, faking effects track the desirability gap between the paired statements (Schulte et al. 2024, seen as an abstract only).

## 2. Candidate contexts (12)

Ratings use P1–P5 from section 1 (5 = best).
DIAMONDS/CAPTION codes: D = Duty, I = Intellect, A = Adversity, M = Mating, O = pOsitivity, N = Negativity, De = Deception, S = Sociality, Ty = Typicality, H = humOr.

### C1. Home & personal space (บ้าน ห้อง และข้าวของของตัวเอง)

Arranging your room or desk, keeping belongings, decorating, light chores, how your space feels.
- **Rationale:** Home is the most common place in daily life. In Matz & Harari's 2-week ESM study it was the most frequent place and the baseline against which the social places shifted states [R7].
  - It is a high-Typicality/Familiarity frame (CAPTION Ty, Situational-Six Familiarity), and rev11 has no such frame [R5][R16].
  - Thai NSO time-use surveys treat household work as one of the main activity groups for everyone aged 6+ [R13].
- **Functions:**
  - Si: things stay where they've always been; comfort of the familiar.
  - Se: what looks and feels good right now.
  - Ne: rearranging, repurposing.
  - Ni: an envisioned "ideal" space.
  - Te: storage systems, efficiency.
  - Ti: figuring out the logic of an arrangement.
  - Fi: the space must feel like "me".
  - Fe: making visitors or housemates comfortable.
  - E/I: home as recharge versus hosting.
  - J/P is very writable.
- **Risks:**
  - Tidiness is Conscientiousness, which is desirable, so J/Te/Si can look like "good adult". Avoid "messy vs tidy" framing and write about *how* people organize, not *whether*.
  - Chores are gendered.
  - People living with parents or in dorms have less control (constraints). Allow "your own corner, room, desk or phone home screen".
- **Ratings:** P1 4 · P2 5 · P3 3 · P4 5 · P5 5 → **22/25**

### C2. Shopping & everyday spending (การซื้อของและการใช้จ่ายในชีวิตประจำวัน)

Choosing products, browsing online or at a market, comparing, impulse buys, buying for others. Not investing.
- **Rationale:** It is close to universal.
  - ETDA 2022 (N = 46,348): 74–88% of Boomers, Gen X, Gen Y and Gen Z shop online.
  - Watching live commerce was the 4th most common online activity (34%) [R20].
  - It is a weak situation: low consequences for any single purchase, many valid strategies, and no single norm.
  - It activates perception (what you notice) and judgement (how you decide) equally well.
- **Functions:**
  - Se: the item grabs you, touch or try.
  - Si: trusted brand or what worked last time.
  - Ne: browsing many options or new things.
  - Ni: "what will I actually use".
  - Te: price per value, reviews, specs.
  - Ti: understanding *why* one is better.
  - Fi: "does it feel like me".
  - Fe: what others would like (gifts), shopping with friends.
  - E/I: shopping alone versus as a social outing.
- **Risks:**
  - Frugality and comparison-shopping read as "smart" (Te/Si desirable). Pair Te with another plausible-and-sensible statement, never with "I buy impulsively".
  - Income: write about process, never amounts.
  - Overlap with "personal taste". Keep taste about *what you like*, and shopping about *how you choose and buy*.
- **Ratings:** P1 5 · P2 5 · P3 3 · P4 5 · P5 4 → **22/25**

### C3. Technology, apps & gadgets (มือถือ แอป และอุปกรณ์)

Setting up a new phone or app, exploring features, fixing a settings problem, choosing apps.
- **Rationale:** Strong Intellect/Complexity cues without academic stakes. It is a natural home for Ti (how it works) versus Te (just get it done), and for Ne (explore every menu) versus Si (keep the familiar layout).
  - Thai average internet use is about 7 h/day [R20].
- **Functions:** Ne, Si, Se (hands-on tinkering), Ni (where tech is heading), Ti, Te, Fi (privacy and values), Fe (helping parents set up LINE, the family group chat).
- **Risks:**
  - Strong age gradient: Boomers 3 h 21 m/day versus Gen Y 8 h 55 m [R20].
  - Tech-savviness may confound N (Openness) and T. Write statements about approach, not skill.
  - Partial overlap with social media and games. Define the frame as "devices, settings, tools", not content.
- **Ratings:** P1 4 · P2 4 · P3 4 · P4 3 · P5 4 → **19/25**

### C4. Daily routine & use of time (กิจวัตรประจำวันและการจัดเวลา)

Mornings, weekends with nothing planned, errands, how you organize a day.
- **Rationale:** It fills Typicality/Familiarity [R5][R16]. It is the best context for Si versus Ne (routine versus variation) and for the J/P axis. It is weakest in situational strength when framed around *unscheduled* time, e.g., a free Saturday or a day off.
- **Functions:**
  - Si: same routine.
  - Ne: vary things.
  - Se: go with what's happening.
  - Ni: a day oriented around one main thing.
  - Te: lists and schedules.
  - Ti: their own logic for sequencing.
  - Fi: do what feels right today.
  - Fe: arrange the day around others.
  - E/I: well covered.
- **Risks:**
  - Punctuality and planning are desirable (C), so Te/J pull. Avoid work-day framings, where employers impose structure (constraints = strong).
  - Overlap with "free time". Make "daily routine" the structural frame (how the day is put together) and "free time & hobbies" the activity-content frame.
- **Ratings:** P1 4 · P2 4 · P3 3 · P4 5 · P5 3 → **19/25**

### C5. Learning something new on your own (เรียนรู้สิ่งใหม่ด้วยตัวเอง)

YouTube tutorials, picking up a skill, a language app, learning a recipe or tool. No school or grades.
- **Rationale:** It keeps the Intellect content of "study" but removes the strong-situation features: no teacher-set clarity, no grades (consequences), full discretion. This follows Judge & Zapata's unstructured-equals-weak logic [R17].
  - It applies to people of any age who are no longer in school, which "study" does not.
- **Functions:**
  - Ne: sample many sources.
  - Ni: grasp the core idea first.
  - Si: step-by-step, repeat until solid.
  - Se: learn by doing, hands-on.
  - Ti: understand the why.
  - Te: practical outcome, fastest path.
  - Fi: learn what's meaningful to me.
  - Fe: learn with or for others.
- **Risks:**
  - Overlap with "study" and "hobbies". Best used as a **replacement or extension** of "study", not an addition.
  - "Curious learner" is mildly desirable (Openness → N pull).
- **Ratings:** P1 5 · P2 5 · P3 4 · P4 4 · P5 2 (as an addition) / 4 (as a replacement for study) → **20–22/25**

### C6. Everyday conversation & chatting (การพูดคุยทั่วไปและแชท)

Small talk, what you like to talk about, group chats, telling stories. Explicitly **not** disagreement.
- **Rationale:** It is the purest Sociality context and the most direct E/I context.
  - Conversation is also where perceiving styles become audible:
    - Ne jumps between topics.
    - Ni goes to the underlying meaning.
    - Se talks about what's happening now.
    - Si tells past stories and details.
  - Judging styles show in what people value in a chat:
    - Ti: precise ideas.
    - Te: getting to the point.
    - Fe: keeping everyone included.
    - Fi: only saying what's genuine.
- **Risks:**
  - It drifts toward A/Fe desirability, because keeping a chat pleasant is a Thai virtue (smooth interpersonal relationships [R18b]).
  - It can slide into the rejected "arguing" frame. Ban debate, criticism and conflict content.
  - Heavy overlap with "friends" and "social media". Frame as *conversation content and style*, not relationships.
- **Ratings:** P1 4 · P2 5 · P3 2 · P4 5 · P5 3 → **19/25**

### C7. Humor & fun (อารมณ์ขัน มุก มีม เรื่องตลก)

What makes you laugh, how you joke, memes.
- **Rationale:** Humor is its own CAPTION dimension, and rev11 has no Humor context [R5].
  - It is a very weak situation: no right answer, low consequences, and low desirability on most poles.
  - Thai "fun-pleasure" orientation is present (Komin [R18b]), so it is engaging.
- **Functions:**
  - Ne: absurd links, wordplay.
  - Ni: irony, satire, layered meaning.
  - Se: physical or slapstick humor, in the moment.
  - Si: callbacks, in-jokes.
  - Ti: clever logic jokes, "the joke has to make sense".
  - Fe: jokes everyone can enjoy, reading the room.
  - Fi: won't laugh at what conflicts with my values.
  - Te is the hardest to write ("humor with a purpose", e.g., using a joke to get a meeting moving; time-efficient humor).
- **Risks:**
  - Te and Si items may feel strained (P2).
  - Fe versus "offensive" framing gives an SD gap, so avoid "offend" wording.
  - Overlap with movies and social media content.
- **Ratings:** P1 5 · P2 3 · P3 4 · P4 5 · P5 4 → **21/25**

### C8. Getting around: commuting & daily trips (การเดินทางในชีวิตประจำวัน)

Daily travel such as BTS, motorbike taxi, bus or car, choosing routes, travel time. Distinct from leisure "travel".
- **Rationale:** It is universal and repeated daily.
  - Thai NSO noted travel as a large, separately coded activity category [R13].
  - It offers strong Se/Si/Ne cues (noticing surroundings, the same route versus new routes) and Te/Ti (optimizing route logic).
  - It is a weak situation for route and activity choice.
- **Functions:** Se, Si, Ne, Ni (lost in thought on the way), Te, Ti. Fi and Fe are thinner: Fe could be giving up a seat or sharing a ride, but giving up a seat for monks or elders is a normative strong cue, so it should be avoided.
- **Risks:**
  - It is constrained by income and mode (constraints facet).
  - It is thin for F.
  - It overlaps travel (low).
- **Ratings:** P1 3 · P2 3 · P3 4 · P4 4 · P5 4 → **18/25**

### C9. Health, body & self-care (สุขภาพ ร่างกาย และการดูแลตัวเอง)

Sleep, small illnesses, trying a health trend, listening to your body.
- **Rationale:** It is near universal.
  - Medical and health services were the #1 online activity in ETDA 2022 (86%) [R20].
  - It is the best context for Si (interoception, "my body usually…") versus Se (raw sensation now) versus Ni (long-term consequences) versus Ne (trying new remedies).
- **Functions:** all perceiving functions very strong; Te (evidence and plans), Ti (understand the mechanism), Fi (what feels right for me), Fe (caring for family health).
- **Risks:**
  - High desirability: health discipline is C/Te/Si "virtue".
  - Sensitive for people with chronic illness.
  - Overlap with sports and food.
  - Medium-strength situation: doctors' advice gives clarity.
- **Ratings:** P1 3 · P2 4 · P3 2 · P4 5 · P5 3 → **17/25**

### C10. Gifts, celebrations & occasions (ของขวัญ งานฉลอง และโอกาสพิเศษ)

Birthdays, New Year, choosing gifts, a party you join or plan. Avoid merit-making and temple framings.
- **Rationale:**
  - Rich F cues: Fi (a gift that expresses me or my real feelings) versus Fe (what they'd like, the occasion's expectations).
  - Rich perceiving cues: Si (tradition), Ne (unexpected gift), Se (atmosphere), Ni (symbolic meaning).
  - Fills Positivity + Sociality.
- **Risks:**
  - Grateful-relationship and reciprocity norms are strong in Thai culture [R18b], so Fe pull is high.
  - Religious festivals bring religio-psychical desirability.
  - Overlap with friends.
- **Ratings:** P1 3 · P2 5 · P3 2 · P4 5 · P5 3 → **18/25**

### C11. Nature & outdoors (ธรรมชาติและกิจกรรมกลางแจ้ง)

Parks, sea or mountain, weather, plants or gardening.
- **Rationale:** Very strong Se (sensory) and Ni (awe, meaning). Si (familiar places), Ne. Low consequences.
- **Risks:**
  - Judging functions are thin (Te: planning a trip; Fi: environmental values, which carry SD).
  - Urban access is unequal.
  - Overlap with travel, sports and pets.
- **Ratings:** P1 5 · P2 2 · P3 4 · P4 3 · P5 3 → **17/25**

### C12. Family & relatives (ครอบครัวและญาติ) — listed for completeness; **not recommended as a free-standing context**

- **Rationale:** It is the most common relationship domain, and Nasello et al. show trait levels shift there [R19]. Self-concept differentiation research uses "self with family" as a core role [R10].
- **Risks:**
  - Komin's grateful-relationship value (reciprocity and gratitude toward parents) and kreng jai make it a **strong situation** for Thai respondents, with a desirable Fe pole [R18][R18b].
  - Family structures vary (orphans, estranged people, people caring for parents), which is a sensitivity problem.
  - It overlaps "relationships".
- If used: restrict to low-stakes sub-frames, such as family meals or a family trip, and keep obligation and elder-care out.
- **Ratings:** P1 2 · P2 4 · P3 1 · P4 4 · P5 2 → **13/25**

## 3. Top picks for rev12

1. **Shopping & everyday spending (C2).**
   - It is the most balanced context for both perceiving (what you notice) and judging (how you decide).
   - It is weak on all four Meyer facets [R2][R3]: low per-purchase consequences, many legitimate strategies, and free choice.
   - It is nearly universal across Thai generations [R20].
   - It also lets "investing" be merged into a broader **money** frame (see section 4), which fixes investing's income bias.
2. **Home & personal space (C1).**
   - It fills the biggest taxonomy hole: Typicality/Familiarity. Every non-US taxonomy has such a dimension, and rev11 has none [R5][R16].
   - Home is the most frequent daily place and the within-person baseline in place–state research [R7].
   - All 8 functions plus J/P and E/I are easy to write.
   - Watch C-desirability: write about organizing style, not tidiness.
3. **Learning something new on your own (C5), as a replacement for or extension of "study".**
   - It keeps the Intellect cues and removes the strong-situation features of school: grades, teacher-defined clarity, and constraints.
   - Judge & Zapata show all traits express better in unstructured, discretionary settings [R17].
   - It makes the context usable for the large adult majority who are no longer in school.
4. **Humor & fun (C7).**
   - The only CAPTION dimension with no rev11 coverage [R5].
   - It is probably the lowest-desirability context available. That makes it valuable as an anchor that is "clean" of the A and C confounds that the MBTI axes carry [R8][R9].
   - Cost: Te and Si items need careful writing. Pilot 2–3 Te statements before committing.
5. **Technology & apps (C3), optional fifth.**
   - It gives a clean Ti versus Te and Ne versus Si contrast without academic or work stakes.
   - Include only if items avoid skill and confidence wording (the age gradient [R20]). Check DIF by age group during calibration.

Runner-up: **Everyday conversation (C6)**. It is the best E/I context, but in Thai culture Fe is the default "good" answer [R18][R18b]. Add it only if E/I coverage in rev11 is thin, and pair statements so that both poles are socially warm.

Collectively, the top picks shift the context mix:
- from Positivity/Sociality-heavy toward Typicality and low-stakes Tasks;
- this covers the three cross-cultural macro-domains (Tasks / Positivity / Negativity) [R5], with mild Negativity coming in through rule R-c mishap items.

## 4. Existing rev11 contexts that may be weak or redundant

| Existing | Problem | Evidence | Suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **free time** + **hobbies** | Almost the same frame: respondents picture the same scenes, which adds no new FOR. | Overlapping place/activity frames in [R7][R12] | **Merge** into "free time & hobbies". Let "daily routine" (if added) carry the structural/J-P content. |
| **relationships** vs **friends** vs **love** | "Relationships" is an umbrella that overlaps both. Respondents may imagine different targets (partner, family, friend), which is exactly the within-person inconsistency FOR is meant to remove. | [R23] Lievens et al. 2008; role-specific self-views differ [R10][R19] | **Split or rename** "relationships" into one specific target (e.g., "close people / housemates"), or drop it. Keep "friends" and "love" as clear frames. |
| **love** | Mating cues are age-dependent and A/Fe-desirable. In Korea, mating loaded with threat/adversity [R5]. Partner context lowered agreeableness and raised neuroticism [R19]. | [R5][R19] | Keep as opt-in, but check DIF and SD per pair. Do not use it for scoring E/I. |
| **work** | A strong situation: clarity, constraints and consequences are high, and employer norms reward Te/Si/J. Extraversion was lowest and conscientiousness highest at work [R19]. | [R2][R3][R17][R19] | Keep, but rewrite toward **discretionary** parts (how you tackle an open task, how you set up your workspace). Weight it lower or check bias toward Te/Si. |
| **study** | Strong situation (grades, teacher rules), and not common for adults. | [R17]; Komin: education valued as a status means [R18b] | **Replace or extend** with "learning something new on your own" (C5). |
| **investing** | Fails P4: income-, age- and literacy-dependent. Prudence is desirable (C/Te). | [R20] shows financial transactions are common, but investing is a subset | **Merge** into "money: spending, saving, buying" together with C2, or keep only as an opt-in sub-frame. |
| **personal taste** | Overlaps art, music, food, movies and clothing, so it gives no distinct frame. | FOR distinctness [R23][R11] | Redefine narrowly as "style: clothes, look, decor", or merge into the arts contexts. |
| **art** vs **music** vs **movies/series** vs **reading** | All are "aesthetic/media consumption". They are fine individually if items differ, but they share Se/Ni/Fi-heavy content, which skews the function balance (rule R-b). | [R19]: openness is stable across contexts, so S–N may not gain from repeated aesthetic frames | Consider merging art into personal taste/style. Make sure Te/Si/Fe items exist in each one. |
| **news** vs **social media** | Overlapping information-intake frames. News invites political disagreement, which is close to the rejected "arguing" frame. | McCrae & Costa / Furnham: T–F ≈ A [R8][R9] | Keep social media. Narrow news to "following what's going on" and ban opinion-conflict items. |
| **beliefs** | A strong normative domain in Thailand (religio-psychical orientation [R18b]). Fi versus Fe pairs become moral choices. | [R18b] | Keep opt-in. Avoid religion and merit framings; focus on "how you form views". Monitor SD. |
| **dreams/aspirations** | Ni/Ne-heavy, and Se/Si are hard to write naturally. Achievement is a low-ranked Thai value, so ambition-type statements are not neutral. | [R18b] | Keep, but audit P2. Consider merging with beliefs as "outlook & future". |
| **pets**, **games**, **sports** | Fail P4 (not universal). Since contexts are opt-in that is acceptable, but small calibration samples will result. | Opt-in caveat (rule R-d) | Keep as opt-in. Do not count them toward the minimum context coverage per function. |
| **general pool** | Untagged generic items are the least valid level of contextualization [R14][R22]. They are still useful as a cross-context anchor for linking item calibrations. | [R14][R22] | Keep as the anchor/link set and do not expand it. |

Net suggestion for the rev12 list (about 23–24 contexts):
- add: Shopping/money, Home & personal space, Learning on your own, Humor, (Technology);
- merge: free time + hobbies; investing → money; study → learning; art → personal taste/style;
- clarify: relationships.
